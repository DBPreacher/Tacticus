# DB Preacher — Voice Guide

**Status: second draft (September 2026).** Written from five scripts in
`Examples/` and corrected by the owner:
- **Space Hulk** and **Top 10 Xenos** are entirely his own writing. **They
  are the voice reference.**
- The **Genestealer Cults** lore was written with a tool, and parts of
  **Laviscus** read the same way (short sentences, em dashes). Use those two
  for structure only, not for sentence style.
- **No lore section by default.** Include lore only when the video needs
  it (e.g. a faction guide).

The five scripts:
- Does Laviscus Do Anything?
- Plan NOW to get Uthar for FREE!
- How to ace the Space Hulk Event
- Top 10 Xenos in Tacticus (2025)
- Beginners Guide to Genestealers Cult

Short quotes from them are used below as examples. Quote the *style*; don't
reuse whole lines in new scripts.

## Who is talking, and to whom

- **A friendly, experienced player, talking to a mixed audience.** Plenty
  of newer players, some veterans. He's knowledgeable, but never lectures.
  - *"I am not a min maxing player. I am not a top meta player. I am here to
    have fun."*
- **Always on the viewer's side, especially free-to-play players.** He cares
  about saving them blackstone, money and tokens.
  - *"make sure you can unlock him completely free of charge"*
  - *"I sadly don't get anything from Snowprint for talking positively about
    the battlepass"*
- **Honest about limits and mistakes:** *"I definitely underrated them last
  year"*, *"taken with a pinch of salt"*, *"let me know if you think
  otherwise!"*
- **Reassures when there's a lot to take in:** *"I'm going to throw a lot of
  names at you but don't panic."*, *"don't despair if you are just getting
  started"*, *"And that is a lot to absorb."*
- **Personal and nostalgic when it fits.** Space Crusade, Space Hulk on the
  Amiga, Aliens 2, Magic: the Gathering zoo decks, "my first ever Diamond III
  character".
- **Has favourites and says so:** *"you all know I love Incisus"*, Tyrant
  Guard, Aleph-Null, Titus "someone you may be sleeping on".

## How it sounds

**Measured on his own scripts** (Space Hulk, Top 10) compared with the
tool-assisted ones (Genestealers, Laviscus):

| | His own | Tool-assisted |
|---|---|---|
| Words per sentence (median) | **23–26** | 13–16 |
| Longest 10% of sentences | **43–46 words or more** | about 25 |
| Em dashes | **none** | up to 16 per script |
| "really" / "just" | **constantly** | rarely |

So:
- **Long, flowing, spoken sentences** joined with "and", "but", "so",
  "because", "and then", "which means". Thoughts pile up the way they do
  when talking:
  - *"Then a few years later, Space Hulk comes out on the Amiga and again,
    I'm totally engrossed, but until the Space Marine video games came out,
    i've been missing that feeling of big boots stomping on a derelict metal
    spaceship until we get our very own Space Hulk event in Tacticus."*
- **No em dashes, and no neat three-part lists** in every sentence. Commas
  and "and" do the joining.
- **Short sentences are rare and saved for a punch** (*"He is just a
  brute."*, *"Really awesome stuff."*).
- **Softeners and intensifiers everywhere:** really, just, pretty, a bit,
  absolutely, honestly, definitely, incredibly, "oh boy", "as heck".
- **Talks the viewer through it** with direct instructions:
  - *"you want to be completing older waves as quickly as possible"*
  - *"Just note that…"*
  - *"The first thing that I want to highlight is…"*
  - *"make sure that you…"*
- **Self-deprecating asides:** *"which wasn't my smartest move"*, *"I
  definitely didn't quite catch on to her usefulness"*.
- **Pop culture and nostalgia** as comparisons: Aliens, the xenomorph,
  Space Marine 2, the Amiga, Springsteen.
- **Jokes about how the characters look,** affectionately: *"may look like
  a mutated shrimp"*, *"That giant nose spike and wrinkly brain gives me
  indigestion"*, *"let's stick him in the remains of a crane!"*
- **British English:** armour, defence, levelling, favourite, prioritise,
  "oh boy", "a bit", "pretty decent", "as heck".
- **Questions start a section,** then get answered straight away:
  - *"So, how do you get this Lord of Excess into your roster?"*
  - *"So who are the best champions to be picking for this event?"*
  - *"So, where does that leave him?"*
- **Frequent linking words:** "So", "Now", "Honestly", "But", "And finally",
  "Alongside that", "Very simply".
- **"The short answer is…",** then the detail: *"The short answer is YES,
  but with a massive asterisk next to his name."*
- **Verdicts are simple, and split by type of player:**
  - *"If you are chasing the meta… He is a Must-Have. If you are a casual
    player… He is a luxury you can probably skip."*
  - *"Hero or Zero."*
- **Numbers are always put in context,** never dumped:
  - *"that totals 265 points, which is actually above the event average of
    197"*
  - *"He sits comfortably in the middle"*
  - *"well below the curve"*
- **Mechanics are explained by what they mean in play,** not by the tooltip.
  Personify the kit:
  - Laviscus "gets jealous" and uses the Outrage for a "kill-steal".
  - Crushing Strike is *"'I'm not chasing you. You come to me.'"*
- **"Champions" and "characters" are both used.** Game terms are
  abbreviated the way players do: LREs, D3, Gold I, TA (Tournament Arena),
  Guild Raids, Guild Wars, Onslaught, Snowprint.

## Humour toolkit (use sparingly: one or two per section, never forced)

- **Nicknames:** the "clobster" (Tyrant Guard), "bellababies" (Bellator's
  Inceptors), "flying shrimp" (Neurothrope), "little space dwarves".
- **Puns, groan-worthy on purpose:** Snotflogga *"doesn't leave mushroom"*,
  *"this Fun Guy"*; Gulgortz *"born in the U.S.A.… dancing in the dark"*.
- **Top 10 sign-off formula:** *"So if you want a [silly description],
  then [character] is the one for you."*
- **Cheeky or innuendo lines, kept light:** Calandis's "leather pants";
  Laviscus *"wants to be slapped around a little bit"*.
- **A dry closing line that ties back to the topic:** *"Until next time, try
  not to step on any suspicious-looking Decoys."*
- **Creed sketches** (see below).

## Recurring moves and phrases

- **Openers:**
  - *"In this video, we'll look at…"*
  - *"So let's dive in."*
  - *"Before we look at the numbers, we need to understand…"*
- **Top 10s:** a drawn-out *"Iiiinnn at Number X"*, with the movement since
  last year (*"down three places"*, *"a new entry"*).
- **The call to action is tied to the value just given:**
  - *"And if this kind of deep-dive planning is helping, then hit subscribe
    as I am constantly building videos to help you save money…"*
  - *"hit that subscribe button because we have a lot of really big
    subscriber targets to hit this year, and I need all your help"*
  - *"You keep me motivated and keep me making content."*
- **A creator code segment** in the middle (`# CTA`): the code, what it
  gives, how long it lasts, and a personal note about the character it's for.
- **Asks for comments with a specific question:** *"Are you building the
  complete faction, or is there one character you use outside the team?"*
- **Points to his own tools:** *"the google planner sheets linked in the
  description below"*, beginner guides, the planner how-to videos.
- **Closers:** *"And that's probably enough for today."*, *"I'll see you next
  time!"*, *"See you next time!"*

## The Creed sketch

A running bit in character reviews: Castellan Creed is the sparring partner.

- **Format:** `ME:` / `CREED:` dialogue lines inside the script.
- **Creed** is a gruff, sardonic Cadian commander who insults the new
  character: *"look at this Fancy Dan… lighting some candles and asking him
  for a slow dance"*, *"Get over here freak"*.
- **ME** is the long-suffering straight man: *"Creed, please don't. Just
  hit him."*
- **Keep it short:** 4–6 lines, then straight back to the numbers.

## Script document conventions

- **Header:** `# Title:`, then `# Youtube Details:`, which holds:
  - **Description:** third person ("DB Preacher looks at… in Warhammer
    40,000: Tacticus"), one or two sentences
  - the `-- Link --` block: planner sheets and how-to videos
  - the `-- My Referral Code --` line
  - optional `-- Attribution --` (the Games Workshop fair-use text)
  - **Timestamps:** filled in after the edit
- **Sections:** `# Hook:`, `# Part N - Name`, `# CTA`, `# Final Word`.
  Character reviews use named sections: Lore, Unlocking, Abilities, Damage
  and Defence, Struggles, Which modes does he fit into?, Mythic Missions,
  Legendary Events.
- **On-screen material** goes in `[On screen only: …]` or `ONSCREEN ONLY`
  blocks: lists, tables and pools the viewer reads while he talks. The
  voiceover then picks out the highlights (*"Huge pool, so I won't read out
  all 32"*).
- **Directions** go in brackets: *("HERO" text over footage…)*.

## Things that don't sound like him

- Corporate or hype phrasing: "game-changer", "unlock your potential",
  "let's unpack", "in today's fast-paced…".
- **The tool rhythm:** short, tidy sentences; em dashes; three-part lists;
  dramatic one-line paragraphs ("Unfortunately, it has only been preparing
  the planet for consumption."). The owner has confirmed this is the tool's
  style, not his.
- **Lore by default.** Only include it when the video needs it.
- Talking down to newer players, or assuming they have every character.
- Absolute claims without a caveat when the data is shaky.
- American spellings.
