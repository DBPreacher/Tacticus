# The Best Faction in Tacticus

*Draft, September 2026. Follow-up to the Roster Battle Map video, so it assumes
people already know the two axes and what a typical character is. Numbers from
`Damage Visualiser/faction-battle-map.html`, built at maxed Mythic with actives
on and everything triggered.*

---

## Hook

The best faction in Tacticus is a two man act, and if you leave either one of
them at home you throw away about eighty percent of what the faction can do.
I'll tell you which two in a bit, but the thing that really got me was how few
factions actually get anything for going pure at all, because I always assumed
that was the whole point of a faction. It really isn't.

## Introduction

So the last video was the Roster Battle Map, which was every character in the
game placed on two axes, how many attacks they need to kill a typical
character, and how many attacks a typical character needs to kill them. That
one did really well and a lot of you asked the same question underneath it,
which was fine, but what about factions? And that's a fair question, because
the map has one very big blind spot. It puts every character in a duel on their
own, with nobody standing next to them, so every single ability that starts
with the words "friendly unit" is worth absolutely nothing on it.

Which is fine when you're comparing characters, because that's the only honest
way to do it. But it's no good at all when you want to know about a faction,
because a faction is five of them playing together and the buffs are the entire
point. So I built the same chart again for whole factions, and that's what
we're looking at today. Same two axes, same scale, so you can put the two pages
side by side and they mean the same thing, but now every character is standing
in a proper team with their own faction around them.

I'll go through what changed, then the thing about faction bonuses that
surprised me, then the sixth character problem, then the bit the model
genuinely doesn't know, and then we'll get to who's actually the best.

## Part 1 - What's different this time

Three things changed and they all pull in the same direction.

The first is that everybody now gets their team mates' buffs, both the
attacking side and the defending side. Every buff, every debuff, every shield
and heal that one of the five hands to another one of the five, it's all in
there and it all counts.

The second one is the interesting one. There's a small number of abilities in
the game with a clause hanging off the end that only works if you're standing
next to one of your own kind. Asmodai hits harder when the enemy is next to
another Dark Angel. Baraqiel's damage doubles the same way. Forcas counts
everyone standing around him. The Winged Prime's Hormagaunts turn up once for
every Synapse ally you brought, so in a Tyranid team they turn up three times as
often. Every one of those was worth precisely zero on the Roster Battle Map,
because there was nobody there. In a mono faction five they're free and they're
permanent, and they're the only real reward for going pure that I can find.

And the third thing is that the yardstick moved. On the old map, a typical
character meant a loose character on their own. Here, every one of the hundred
and seventeen gets their own faction's buffs first, and then they all get
measured against each other. So a faction isn't being scored against a bunch of
lone characters, it's being scored against other factions playing together,
which is the fight it's actually going to have.

## Part 2 - The two kinds of faction bonus, and one of them is a lie

Right, so this is the bit I got wrong before I built it.

There are two completely different things that get called a faction bonus and
they really do need keeping apart. The first one is a signature trait, so Get
Stuck In for the Orks, Blessings of Khorne for the World Eaters, Act of Faith
for the Sisters. Eleven of the twenty one factions have one.

And here's the thing about them. Every character carries their signature trait
everywhere they go. Ragnar doesn't lose anything by being in a mixed team, and
an Ork in your Guild Raid squad still has Get Stuck In. So a signature trait is
already baked into that character's own numbers on the old map, and going pure
adds exactly nothing to it. It's a faction flavour, it isn't a faction reward,
and I think a lot of us have been reading it the wrong way round.

The actual reward is the second kind, the locked clause, and it's much rarer
than you'd think. Atlacoya's active does Direct damage but only if he's next to
a friendly Custodes. Aesoth's passive reaches further if a friendly Custodes
uses an active. Thaumachus shields the closest ally and prefers Thousand Sons.
Those conditions are annoying and situational in a mixed team, and in a mono
faction five they're just permanently on, for free, forever.

So when you go looking for which factions actually gain something from being
run pure, don't count the ones with a nice trait. Count the ones with clauses.
It's a much shorter list.

## Part 3 - The sixth character, and the Space Wolves problem

Now, twelve factions have exactly five characters, so there's no decision to
make. You field them, that's the team, off you go. Nine factions have six, so
you've got to leave somebody on the bench, and Adeptus Astartes have three and
can't field a team at all, so they're sat in the table with a dash next to them.

On the chart, a faction sits on its best five and then there's a little line
trailing behind it with dots on, and each dot is one of the other fives you
could have picked. A faction with no line has no choice to make. A faction with
a long line is telling you that the choice is the whole story.

And oh boy, the Space Wolves. Their line is enormous, it's off on its own, it's
nothing like anybody else's. Their best five comes in at nought point six two
attacks, which is the best number on the chart, and their worst five is three
point two one. That's more than five times worse for swapping one character.
Nobody else is close, the next biggest swing is the Tau at a bit over double.

What's going on is that the Space Wolves are really a two man act. Baldr heals
somebody and that heal hands them a big chunk of crit chance and crit damage,
and Ragnar is the one character in the game who does the most horrible things
with it. Put those two together and the whole faction rides on it. Bench Ragnar
and you drop to three point two one. Bench Baldr and you drop to one point
nought two, which is still decent but it isn't first place any more. So the
answer for the Space Wolves isn't really who's the sixth best Space Wolf, it's
don't you dare break up that pair.

One more thing on benching, and I think this is genuinely useful. The character
you leave out to attack is usually not the character you leave out to defend.
For the Ultramarines you bench Bellator if you want damage and Certus if you
want to survive. For the Necrons it's Makhotep or Thutmose. Seven of the nine
change their mind depending on the job, and only the Aeldari and the Dark
Angels bench the same person either way. So if you've been running one fixed
faction team for both, you've probably been running the wrong one half the
time.

## Part 4 - What this doesn't know, and you should hear it from me

I want to be really straight about this because it's Arena, and Arena is
different.

The model has no idea about turn order. None. It doesn't know who moves first,
it doesn't know that the team who goes first can focus somebody down before
they ever get to answer, and in Arena that's frequently the entire game. So
what you're looking at is a ranking of what a faction can do, and not a
prediction of who wins.

Underneath it, it's also still a one on one measure. Five characters buff each
other, absolutely, but the actual fight being modelled is one attacker hitting
one defender. There's no focus fire in there, nobody dies halfway through and
stops answering, nothing gets reinforced. I've kept it that way on purpose,
because it's the only reason these numbers line up with the old map at all, but
it does mean you should read the chart as a league table and not as a result.

And the machines. Eleven factions get a Machine of War, and there's a toggle
for it, but six of those machines are defensive, like the Galatian and the
Exorcist and the Storm Speeder, and I haven't read their Mythic abilities into
the model yet. So a machine never moves the toughness axis at the moment. That
one's on me and it's on the list.

## Part 5 - The stuff I found out on the way

A few things fell out of this that I wasn't looking for.

The Aeldari and the Emperor's Children have no defensive support at all.
Nothing. Not a heal, not a shield, not one point of damage reduction handed to
anybody. Every other faction in the game gains something on the defensive side
by grouping up and those two gain exactly nought percent, and you can see it on
the chart because they're both sat well over on the squishy side.

Then there's how completely unrelated the two kinds of synergy are. The Tau
gain a ridiculous amount by playing together on the attacking side, more than
three times better, and then they're fourteenth for toughness. The Ultramarines
are the opposite, they gain about a quarter on attack and they more than double
on defence. The Sisters gain nought point one percent attacking and forty seven
percent defending. A faction is built for one job or the other, pretty much
across the board, and I honestly didn't expect it to be that clean.

The World Eaters do not own a single ranged weapon. Not one, across the whole
faction. The Thousand Sons are the mirror of that and every single one of their
six is a shooter. And eleven of the twenty one have nobody at all who heals or
repairs, which really shows up when you put the toughness numbers next to each
other.

## Part 6 - So who is the best faction in Tacticus

Right, the actual answer.

For pure damage it's the Space Wolves at nought point six two, with the Tau
right behind them at nought point six three, and those two are miles clear of
everybody. Third is the Aeldari at one point nought five, so there's a proper
gap there.

For toughness it's the Death Guard at three point six one, then the
Ultramarines at three point five, then the Custodes at three point four five,
and none of those three are anywhere near the top for damage.

So if you want one answer, it's the Space Wolves. They're first for damage and
they're still sixth of twenty one for toughness, and nobody else manages to be
near the top of both. The Custodes are the honest second place and they're
probably the more forgiving team, because they're sixth and third instead of
first and sixth, and they don't fall apart if you bench the wrong person.

And at the bottom, and I'm sorry about this, it's the Astra Militarum. Twentieth
for damage, eighteenth for toughness, and the best five they've got is the one
where you leave Castellan Creed at home. My own sparring partner, the character
I've used to test every single model in this project, and the maths says bench
him. I'm not doing it. I want that on record. But the maths says it.

## Outro

So that's the Faction Battle Map. Link's down below, it's the same deal as last
time, one file, nothing to install, open it and click about. You can flip the
Machine of War on and off, you can pick the five for attacking or for
defending, and the panel on the side tells you what each faction has got and
what it hasn't.

If you want the thing to take away from this, it's that a signature trait is
not a reason to go pure, and a locked clause is. And if you're a Space Wolves
player, keep Baldr and Ragnar next to each other and don't let anybody tell you
otherwise.

Let me know which faction you run and whether the chart matches what you see in
