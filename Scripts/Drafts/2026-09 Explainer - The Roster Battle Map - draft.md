# Hook:
For the last two years, every time a new character has arrived in Tacticus, I've done the same thing. I've taken them into a test battle, pointed them at poor old Castellan Creed, and let them hit him in the face.

It's been a good bit. Creed complains, Creed gets hurt, and you get to see the numbers. But the more characters we've had, the more I've realised that one man with a Las pistol can't tell us everything we need to know.

So today I want to show you what's replacing him. It's a map of every single character in the game, all 117 of them, showing how hard they hit and how hard they are to kill. And it works at Gold, Diamond III and Mythic, with or without gear.

But first, somebody needs to be told. So let's dive in.

So why am I doing this? Honestly, Creed was a great punching bag. When I tested how hard a new character hit *him*, the results lined up almost perfectly with how they do against the whole roster.

The problem was the other direction. When Creed was the one doing the hitting, his Las pistol only has a 10% pierce ratio. So anyone with big armour looked like an absolute fortress, just because Creed personally couldn't scratch them. Kut Skoden came out as one of the toughest characters in the game. Against everyone else, he's pretty much middle of the pack.

So rather than one man, we now test every character against every other character in the game. And that gives us two very simple numbers.

# Part 2 - The two numbers

Both numbers are counted in attacks, because that's how we all think about Tacticus anyway.

The first is **Damage**: how many attacks does this character need to kill a typical character?

The second is **Toughness**: how many attacks does a typical character need to kill *them*?

[On screen only: "Damage = attacks they need to kill a typical character" / "Toughness = attacks a typical character needs to kill them"]

And when I say "typical", I don't mean one character. I work out the fight against all 117 characters in the game and take the one right in the middle, so half the roster does better and half does worse. That way one weird matchup can't mess up the answer.

So when I say something like "Kharn kills a typical character in about one and a half attacks", that is exactly what I mean, and it's why he's been sitting at the top of every damage list since he arrived.

# Part 3 - Reading the map

[On screen: Map view. Press **3**]

So here is the map. Every dot is a character.

The higher they are, the harder they hit. The further right they are, the harder they are to kill.

That splits the map into four corners.
- Top left, you have your **glass cannons**: they hit very hard, but a stiff breeze knocks them over.
- Top right is where you want to be: **hits hard and hard to kill**.
- Bottom right are the **tanks**: they'll outlast everyone, they just won't kill much while they do it.
- And bottom left is the **weak stat line** corner… which is a polite way of saying these characters really need their kit to do anything.

The dashed lines are the middle of the roster, so anyone above or to the right of them is better than average.

The colour tells you the alliance, just like the in-game icons: gold for Imperial, red for Chaos and light blue for Xenos. And the shape tells you what their best attack is: a circle for melee and a diamond for ranged.

When I pin a character, I get their card on the right. Their stats, their damage and toughness scores with their rank out of 117, what their passive and active are actually doing, their gear, and yes… a little sparring line against Creed. He's retired, not dead.

# Part 4 - Attack and Defence

[On screen: press **1** for Attack, then **2** for Defence]

If the map is too much to take in at once, and trust me, with 117 characters it can be, I can split it in two.

The **Attack view** keeps the damage numbers exactly where they are and just spreads everyone out left to right, so you can actually read the names. Top of the pile is the damage elite; bottom of the pile… we'll come to him.

The **Defence view** does the same thing for toughness, spreading everyone top to bottom.

And whenever a new character arrives, this is how you'll see me do it. They drop onto the map, and within a couple of seconds you can see exactly where they sit against everyone you already own.

# Part 5 - The switches

Now, Tacticus is a ridiculously complicated game, so a single number was never going to be enough. That's why the map has a few switches, and I'll show you why each one matters.

## Progression
[On screen: press **P** to cycle Gold → Diamond III → Mythic]

First is progression. There are three settings:
- **Gold**: Epic, eight stars, Gold I.
- **Diamond III**: Legendary with the wings, which is what I've always used for Creed's testing.
- **Mythic**: fourteen stars, Adamantine II, with abilities up to level 60 and their relic.

Each one is compared against the roster at that same level, so Mythic Kharn is being measured against Mythic everyone else.

## Traits
[On screen: Boss Gulgortz pinned, press **T**. `#obs&view=attack&tier=d3&sel=Gulgortz`]

Next is traits. By default, I only count the traits that always work, like Terminator Armour, Mk X Gravis or Parry. But lots of traits only work if something happens first. Crushing Strike only works if you haven't moved. Heavy Weapon, the same. Rapid Assault is only your first attack.

So there's a second setting called **All triggered**, where everyone gets their conditions met. Watch Boss Gulgortz here. On his always-on traits he's 68th for damage. Let him stand still and get Crushing Strike and he rockets up to 14th. That's the difference between a good Gulgortz player and a bad one.

## Ability level and the active
[On screen: Kharn pinned. Press **L**, then **A**]

Passives are always counted, because they are always part of the character. The active ability has its own switch, because you choose when to use it.

And the level matters a lot. At Diamond III with level 36 skills, Kharn's active barely changes anything, because his normal attack is already so strong. Push his skills to level 50 and suddenly he kills a typical character in under one attack. One. That's the power of levelling your skills, and it's a big reason I'll keep showing both.

It works the other way too. Aesoth's active gives him 66% damage reduction for a turn, and with it switched on he jumps from 7th to 2nd toughest in the game.

## Gear
[On screen: Judh pinned. Press **G**]

And finally, gear. With no gear, nobody can crit or block, which really punishes some characters. So the standard setting gives everyone the best items they can equip for their tier.

Judh here is the perfect example. She has two crit items, so with no gear she's 111th for damage. Give her the gear she'd actually have and she's 19th. Meanwhile poor Bellator goes the other way, because crits ignore his Mk X Gravis armour, and he drops from 9th toughest to 37th.

# Part 6 - So does it actually work?

[On screen: Attack view, Diamond III. Press **1**]

So how do I know this isn't just a pretty picture?

Well, let's look at the very bottom of the damage list. Right at the bottom, 117th out of 117, is Ammuk. And the reason is simple. His attack is one single hit of Physical damage, and Physical only has a 1% pierce ratio. Against a typical character's armour, he needs about 22 attacks to kill them. Twenty-two. That is absolutely woeful, and honestly, seeing him at the bottom is how I knew the map was working.

At the other end, Tyrant Guard only looks decent on his stats alone, around 32nd. But switch his passive on, which remember, is always on, and the clobster shoots up to 4th toughest in the game. Which is exactly what anyone who has played an LRE with him already knew.

And Re'vas is my favourite example. On her stat line she's near the bottom for damage. Give her level 50 skills and let her use her active and she's in the top 10. That is Re'vas in a nutshell: her stats are nothing special, her kit is everything.

# Part 7 - What it doesn't tell you

Now, I have to be honest with you. This is a comparison, not a fight simulator.

It doesn't know about terrain. It doesn't count summons, so Bellator's bellababies and Creed's Guardsmen don't help their scores. It doesn't count buffs that only help your *other* characters, so a support like Abaddon or Calgar will always look a bit worse on here than they are in your team. And it uses average dice rolls, so the occasional monster crit won't show up.

So if a support character sits low on the map, that doesn't mean they're bad. It means they're not a solo fighter, and I'll always tell you when that's the case.
