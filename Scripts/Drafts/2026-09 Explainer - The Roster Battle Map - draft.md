So rather than testing everyone against one man, I now test every character against every other character in the game, and that gives us two really simple numbers.

# Part 2 - The two numbers

Both numbers are counted in attacks, because that's how we all think about Tacticus anyway when we're lining up a kill.

The first is **Damage**, which is how many attacks this character needs to kill a typical character, and the second is **Toughness**, which is how many attacks a typical character needs to kill *them*.

[On screen only: "Damage = attacks they need to kill a typical character" / "Toughness = attacks a typical character needs to kill them"]

Now when I say typical, I don't mean one character like Creed. I work out the fight against all 117 characters and take the result right in the middle, so half the roster does better than that and half does worse, which means one weird matchup can't mess the whole thing up.

So when I say that Kharn kills a typical character in about one and a half attacks, that's exactly what I mean, and it's also why he's been sat at the top of pretty much every damage list since he arrived.

# Part 3 - Reading the map

[On screen: Map view. Press **3**]

So here is the map, and every dot on it is a character.

The first thing I want to point out is that the higher up a character is, the harder they hit, and the further to the right they are, the harder they are to kill, which splits the map into four corners.

Top left are your **glass cannons**, who hit really hard but fall over if you look at them funny, and top right is where you really want to be, because these characters **hit hard and are hard to kill**. Bottom right are the **tanks**, who will outlast just about everyone but aren't going to kill a lot while they do it, and bottom left is the **weak stat line** corner, which is a polite way of saying these characters really need their kit to do anything at all.

The dashed lines are the middle of the roster, so anyone above or to the right of them is better than average, and the colours are the alliances, just like the in-game icons, so gold for Imperial, red for Chaos and light blue for Xenos. The shape just tells you what their best attack is, with a circle for melee and a diamond for ranged.

And when I pin a character, I get their card on the right with their stats, their damage and toughness scores and where they rank out of 117, what their passive and active are actually doing, their gear, and yes, there is still a little sparring line against Creed at the bottom, because he's retired, not dead.

# Part 4 - Attack and Defence

[On screen: press **1** for Attack, then **2** for Defence]

If the map is a bit much to take in all at once, and trust me with 117 characters it really can be, then I can split it into two.

The **Attack view** keeps everyone's damage exactly where it is and just spreads them out left to right so you can actually read the names, and you want to be looking at the top of the pile for the real damage dealers, while the bottom of the pile is someone we'll come back to in a minute.

The **Defence view** does exactly the same thing for toughness, but spreads everyone out from top to bottom instead.

And this is how you'll see me use it whenever a new character arrives, because they'll just drop onto the map and within a couple of seconds you'll be able to see exactly where they sit against every character you already own.

# Part 5 - The switches

Now Tacticus is a ridiculously complicated game, as we all know, so one number was never going to be enough, and that's why the map has a few switches that I want to show you, because each one really does change the picture.

## Progression
[On screen: press **P** to cycle Gold → Diamond III → Mythic]

The first is progression, and there are three settings. **Gold** is an Epic character at eight stars and Gold I, **Diamond III** is a Legendary character with the wings, which is what I've always used for Creed's testing, and **Mythic** is fourteen stars at Adamantine II, with abilities up to level 60 and their relic as well.

Just note that each one is compared against the rest of the roster at that same level, so Mythic Kharn is being measured against Mythic everyone else, not against your Gold characters.

## Traits
[On screen: Boss Gulgortz pinned, press **T**. `#obs&view=attack&tier=d3&sel=Gulgortz`]

Next up is traits, and by default I only count the ones that always work, like Terminator Armour, Mk X Gravis or Parry. But a lot of traits only work when something happens first, so Crushing Strike only works if you haven't moved, Heavy Weapon is the same, and Rapid Assault is only your first attack of the battle.

So there's a second setting called **All triggered**, where every character gets their conditions met, and if you watch Boss Gulgortz here, he's 68th for damage on his always-on traits, but let him stand still and get that Crushing Strike and oh boy, he rockets all the way up to 14th. That's really the difference between a good Gulgortz player and a bad one, and it's a mistake I've definitely made myself more times than I'd like to admit.

## Ability level and the active
[On screen: Kharn pinned. Press **L**, then **A**]

Passives are always switched on, because they're always part of the character, but the active ability has its own switch, because you're the one choosing when to use it.

And the level of those skills really matters, because at Diamond III with level 36 skills, Kharn's active barely changes anything at all, as his normal attack is already so strong. But push his skills to level 50 and suddenly he kills a typical character in under one attack, and that's a pretty good reason to keep levelling your skills if you've been putting it off.

It works the other way round too, because Aesoth's active gives him 66% damage reduction for a turn, and with that switched on he jumps from 7th toughest in the game all the way up to 2nd.

## Gear
[On screen: Judh pinned. Press **G**]

And finally there's gear, because with no gear nobody can crit or block, and that really punishes some characters, so the standard setting gives everyone the best items they can equip at their level.

Judh is the perfect example here, because she has two crit items, so with no gear she's all the way down in 111th for damage, but give her the gear she'd actually have and she shoots up to 19th. Poor Bellator goes completely the other way though, because crits ignore his Mk X Gravis armour, and he drops from 9th toughest down to 37th, which surprised me a bit when I first saw it.

# Part 6 - So does it actually work?

[On screen: Attack view, Diamond III. Press **1**]

So how do I know this isn't just a pretty picture? Well, let's look at the very bottom of the damage list, because right down at 117th out of 117 is Ammuk.

And the reason is really simple, because his attack is one single hit of Physical damage, and Physical only has a 1% pierce ratio, so against a typical character's armour he needs about 22 attacks to kill them. Twenty-two attacks! That is absolutely woeful, and honestly, seeing him right at the bottom is how I knew the map was actually working.

At the other end, you all know how much I love the clobster, and Tyrant Guard only looks decent on his stats alone at around 32nd, but switch on his passive, which remember is always on, and he shoots up to 4th toughest in the game, which is exactly what anyone who's taken him into a legendary event already knew.

And Re'vas is probably my favourite example of the lot, because on her stat line she's right near the bottom for damage, but give her level 50 skills and let her use her active and she's up in the top 10. That really is Re'vas in a nutshell, because her stats are nothing special at all, it's her kit that does everything.

# Part 7 - What it doesn't tell you

Now I do have to be honest with you here, because this is a comparison and not a fight simulator.

It doesn't know anything about terrain, and it doesn't count summons, so Bellator's bellababies and Creed's Guardsmen don't help their scores at all. It also doesn't count buffs that only help your other characters, so a support like Abaddon or Calgar will always look a bit worse on here than they really are in your team, and it uses average dice rolls, so the occasional monster crit won't show up either.

So if a support character sits quite low on the map, that doesn't mean they're a bad character, it just means they're not a solo fighter, and I'll always make sure to tell you when that's the case.
