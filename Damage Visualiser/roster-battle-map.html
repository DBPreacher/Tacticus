Reading the chart

Damage: higher is better
How many of their attacks it takes to kill a typical character.
Toughness: further right is better
How many attacks a typical character needs to kill them.
What "typical character" means
Not one character. Each fight is worked out against every one of the 117 characters, and the middle result is used: half the roster does better than that, half worse. So one unusual matchup can't skew the answer.
Always-on traits
Only traits that always work, whatever happens in the battle:
  • Terminator Armour: −75% on the first attack each turn (not Psychic/Direct)
  • Mk X Gravis: damage goes through Armour twice
  • Parry: melee attacks against it score −1 hit
  • Terrifying: −30% damage from melee attacks
  • Martial Ka'tah: −20% damage from normal attacks
  • Camouflage: ranged attacks against it score −1 hit
  • Beast Slayer: +20% melee damage vs Big Targets and Vehicles
All triggered
All of the always-on traits, plus every trait whose condition is met:
  • Rapid Assault: +25% (first attack of the battle)
  • Heavy Weapon: +25% ranged (hasn't moved)
  • Crushing Strike: +50% melee (hasn't moved)
  • Ranged Specialist: +33% ranged (started away from enemies)
  • Prioritised Efficiency: +25% dealt, −25% taken
  • Weaver of Fates: maximum damage roll
  • Contagions of Nurgle: −20% target Armour in melee
  • Blessings of Khorne: at 4 kills, +12% dealt, −32% Psychic taken
  • Shadow in the Warp: −25% Psychic damage from Psykers
  • Camouflage: −2 hits vs range 3+ weapons
  • At their average: Daemon and Beast Slayer blocks, Get Stuck In and Let the Galaxy Burn extra hits
  • Passives that need charging or moving: Kut Skoden, Deathleaper, Ragnar, Tanksmasha, Toth's revive, Wrask's shield
One enemy turn = 5 attacks
Toughness assumes a full enemy team of 5 focuses on the character. Effects that last one round, from an active, only protect against those first 5 attacks, then wear off. Terminator Armour and other "first attack each turn" effects come back every 5 attacks. Characters whose active is limited this way: .
Passives: always on
Every character's passive counts at the chosen ability level, when it affects their own attacks or protects them. Buffs for allies, reactions and summons don't count, except bodyguards that take the hits for them (Kell, Geminae Superia).
Gear: none or standard
Standard gear is each character's own item slots filled with the best Legendary items they can equip, at top level, including boosters. No relics. Crits and blocks count at their average: a chain starts on the first hit and carries on while each roll succeeds. Crits add Crit Damage before armour and skip Mk X Gravis, so Gravis tanks lose some toughness. Abilities that "cannot Crit" don't. Crit and block parts of kits (Titus, Ragnar, Lysander, Trajann, Arjac, Dreir and others) only count with gear on.
Active ability: on or off
With it on, everyone opens with their active. For damage, it's used if it beats a normal attack; damage to other enemies, summons and buffs don't count. For toughness, its protection counts: damage reduction, heals, and suppressing or stunning enemies (an area effect covers the enemy turn; a single target only its first attack). At Winged D3 a level 36 active rarely beats a normal attack; at level 50 most do.

Who is where
Stats are from the tacticustable.com game data at Winged D3 with no gear. The rules are in Damage Visualiser/DAMAGE_MODEL.md and each active ability's handling is in active_abilities.csv. The numbers are for comparing characters, not predicting an exact fight.
