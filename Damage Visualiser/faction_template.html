Reading the chart

Damage: higher is better
How many attacks the five need to kill a typical character, counted per member. Five attacking together kill something in 1 ÷ the sum of their rates, and multiplying that by five puts it back on the same scale as the Roster Battle Map. The five is a fixed scale rather than a head count, so it stays at five with a Machine of War helping and the machine simply makes the number better. Lower on the number, higher on the chart.
Toughness: further right is better
How many attacks it takes to kill the typical member of the five, which is not the easiest one to kill. Focus fire takes a team apart one at a time, so the attacks needed to wipe all five is simply the five numbers added up, whatever order they die in — and this axis is that total divided by five, to keep it on the same scale as the Roster Battle Map. The panel gives the whole-team figure as well.
What "typical character" means here
Not a loose character. Every one of the 117 is first given its own faction's buffs, and then they are measured against each other. So a faction is being judged against other factions playing together, which is the fight it will actually have. It is the Roster Battle Map's typical character moved up a level.
The bar behind a badge
Nine factions have six characters and must bench one. The badge sits on their best five and the bar runs to their worst, along whichever axis is being ranked, so its length is exactly what benching the wrong character costs. Twelve factions have five characters and no bar at all, which is its own answer: there is no decision to make. The one left out is named in the panel, and for seven of the nine it is not the same character for attacking as for defending.
Why these are not the Roster Battle Map's numbers
They should not be. On the map every character fights a plain roster, nobody buffed. Here every one of the 117 has its own faction standing round it, so the enemies hit much harder and are much harder to kill. Toughness drops by about 18% across the roster as a result, and where a faction has no defensive support of its own it drops a great deal more: Hascule is 3.65 attacks on the map and 2.28 here, because the Emperor's Children give each other nothing on that side while everyone they face is being shielded and healed. Where the support is strong it goes the other way, and Imospekh more than doubles. The grey figure beside each character in the panel is their map number, so the gap is always in front of you.
Both axes assume focus fire
Damage has the five all hitting one enemy, and Toughness has a full enemy team of five hitting one of yours. It is deliberately the same fight from both ends, and it is what Arena actually is: you remove a threat before it gets to act. Killing five enemies that way simply takes five times as long, so counting one or counting five gives exactly the same ranking. It would only change if the five split up and took an enemy each, because then the slowest member sets the pace rather than the fastest — and a faction carried by one monster would look far worse. That is a real difference: the Leagues of Votann are 7th here and would be 19th, which is Uthar doing the work.
A healer does not attack
You cannot heal and attack, so a heal is treated as a repeatable active: the eleven characters with a heal or repair action either spend the turn on it or spend it swinging, and the model keeps whichever is better for the axis being asked about. It matters most for Baldr, whose Healing Balms hands the unit he heals a great deal of Crit, and it costs the Space Wolves first place on damage. Worth knowing: under focused fire at this level 112 of the 117 die inside a single enemy turn, so a heal that arrives once a turn usually arrives too late, which is why so few fives choose to heal.
Synergy: what going pure is worth
The same five measured against the same yardstick with their buffs on and with them off. It counts the mates' buffs on both sides, and the faction-locked clauses that only work next to one of their own — Asmodai and Baraqiel beside a Dark Angel, Forcas counting who is next to him, the Winged Prime's Hormagaunts arriving once for every Synapse ally. On the Roster Battle Map every one of those is worth exactly nothing, because there is nobody standing beside them.
A signature trait is not a synergy
Get Stuck In, Blessings of Khorne, Act of Faith and the rest are carried by each character wherever it fights, so they are already in that character's own numbers and they are no reward for going pure. Ten factions have one, eleven do not, and it makes no difference to the gain shown here. The reward for going pure is the locked clause, and it is much rarer.
Machine of War
Eleven of the 21 have one, and with it on you get its Mythic ability and the attack it fires every round, counted as a sixth attacker. Six of those Mythic abilities are a percentage on damage; the other five are defensive and are counted too, so a machine moves both axes. Four of them take a flat 20% off everything the five takes, and the Exorcist's Shield of Faith is worth half a character's Health every turn. A machine is a sixth attacker, never a sixth body for the enemy to kill. What is still missing is some of what the machines do: each has two active abilities and the model fires only one of them, and the ones that summon something are not counted at all. The Galatian is the worst case, because neither of its actives is counted.
Turn order is not modelled at all
In Arena it is frequently the whole game: who moves first, who gets to focus somebody down before they answer. None of that is here. This page ranks what a faction can do, not who goes first.
"Attacks to kill" is still a one-on-one measure
The buffs are a team, but the fight underneath is still one attacker against one defender. There is no focus fire, nobody dies mid-fight and stops answering, and nothing is reinforced. The simplification is kept on purpose, because it is what makes these numbers comparable to the Roster Battle Map — but it is why the chart should be read as a ranking and not as a prediction.
Where a buff lands
A buff that cannot reach everyone goes to the members who use it best: the hitters for an attack buff, the fragile for a shield. Three team-mates count as adjacent, the same number every adjacency question in this project answers to.
